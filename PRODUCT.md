# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
Hiring managers and recruiters come first. They arrive from a referral note,
a resume link, or LinkedIn, usually on a laptop between meetings, and decide
in about 30 seconds whether Abdul is worth a conversation: what Abdul builds,
at what level, and how to reach out. ML engineers and researchers come
second, arriving through open-source work, research, or (later) writing,
with more time and more interest in the technical detail.

## Product Purpose
A personal site for an ML engineer at Google (Play Ads): show the work and
the thinking behind it, carry the resume and contact links, and leave room
for writing later. Success: a hiring manager can say what Abdul works on
after one screen, and reaches the resume, LinkedIn, or email in one click.

## Brand Personality
Curious, grounded, warm. The voice is a thoughtful engineer talking plainly
about real work, and about hiking, running, and drums. Calm and unhurried,
confident without selling.

## Anti-references
- SaaS landing pages: big hero, stat blocks, gradient buttons, card grids.
- Dev-terminal clichés: dark mode by default, neon, monospace everywhere,
  fake prompts.
- Generic resume templates: timeline dots, skill bars, percentage meters.
- Heavy animation: parallax, scroll-jacking, elements flying in.

## Design Principles
1. Show the work, don't sell it: concrete systems and outcomes in plain
   language, no adjectives doing the lifting.
2. A person, not a profile: life outside work gets real space.
3. Scannable in 30 seconds, rewarding at 3 minutes.
4. Public-safe by default: no revenue figures, no job-search language,
   nothing from uncleared side projects.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA. Honors prefers-reduced-motion. Fully keyboard-navigable,
with visible focus. Photos carry descriptive alt text.
